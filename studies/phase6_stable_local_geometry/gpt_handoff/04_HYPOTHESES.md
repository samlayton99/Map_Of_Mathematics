# 04 — Registered Hypotheses for the Next Research Push

These hypotheses are ordered by importance.

## H1 — Hierarchy-restoration hypothesis

A typed occurrence hierarchy with generated-owner collapse will reduce:

- artificial-jump rate;
- universal hub concentration;
- refactoring instability;

while preserving or improving important-move recall at equal visible-edge budget versus flat top-\(k\).

**Falsify if:** hierarchical views do not improve at least two of those outcomes or materially hide legitimate definitions/theorems.

## H2 — Rarity-as-surrogate hypothesis

The tail advantage of global rarity partly compensates for proof-term nesting lost by flattening.

**Test:** compare ordinal/local stable ranking on:

- flat candidate lists;
- hierarchical sibling sets.

**Support if:** the rarity advantage shrinks substantially after hierarchy restoration.

## H3 — Delta-is-span hypothesis

`delta_depth` predicts abstraction span but is not sufficient for shortcut quality.

**Test:** compare shortcut judgments using:

- delta alone;
- cited depth alone;
- \((d_c,\Delta d)\);
- relative span;
- role plus span;
- contextual interface anchoring.

**Expected:** the two-dimensional/typed models beat delta alone.

## H4 — Vertical-rendering hypothesis

Rendering high-span low-cited-depth edges as vertical drill-down portals, rather than lateral neighbours, reduces false global shortcuts without loss of foundation traceability.

## H5 — Stable-consensus hypothesis

A normalized Condorcet/Copeland score over append-safe local voters can provide a useful first-move confidence signal without fitted weights.

Candidate voters:

- occurrence role;
- proof-introduced status;
- cited depth;
- arity;
- isProof;
- application nesting;
- generated-owner status;
- contextual anchoring.

Do not include live rarity in the compliant variant.

## H6 — Local-magnitude hypothesis

Append-safe local magnitudes can recover some tail performance currently supplied by rarity.

Candidates:

- rooted subterm size/height;
- number of local child obligations;
- application-tree coverage;
- proof/body compression ratio;
- unique local cone contribution;
- instantiation specificity;
- contextual interface overlap.

## H7 — Definition-lane hypothesis

Separating definition occurrences into move, concept, and infrastructure lanes improves navigation cleanliness and explanatory coverage over one all-kinds flat ranking.

## H8 — Scale-stationarity hypothesis

One of the following yields more stable calibration across depth bands and library versions:

- absolute span;
- relative span;
- cited-depth ratio;
- within-proof span rank;
- local hierarchy level.

Do not assume success. Measure distributional collapse.

## H9 — Local/global two-channel hypothesis

A navigator using:

- stable local features;
- plus versioned global fields;

outperforms either alone on current navigation while preserving local extension invariance.

## H10 — Condorcet confidence hypothesis

Proofs with a stable Condorcet winner among local voters have better navigation cleanliness and lower disagreement than proofs without one.

Use it as a confidence annotation even if it does not define the full order.
