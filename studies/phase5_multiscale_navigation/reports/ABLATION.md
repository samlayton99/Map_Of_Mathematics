# Which signal actually does the work — ablation

**Status: EXPLORATORY.** Computed on the sealed TEST-R labels *after*
unsealing. It generates hypotheses; it cannot promote anything, and TEST-R is
now burned for these specific questions. Round 2 needs a fresh sample.

Prompted by a challenge from the project lead: "rarity will not fly... I
believe depth is the right idea... I don't think you are explaining the whole
story." Both halves of that were fair.

---

## 1. My earlier analysis used the wrong instrument

`FAILURE_ANALYSIS.md` decomposed the promoted score by **variance share** and
concluded it was "a rarity score with decoration", with role and statement
dismissed at 5.7% and 2.5%.

Variance share is not importance. The correct test is to remove each factor
and measure what breaks.

**Leave-one-out from the promoted `role × statement × depth × rarity` (0.871):**

| drop this factor | score | cost of dropping |
|---|---|---|
| **role** | 0.836 | **0.035** |
| rarity | 0.849 | 0.022 |
| statement | 0.869 | 0.002 |
| **depth** | **0.878** | **−0.007** (dropping it *helps*) |

**Role is the largest contributor, not rarity.** A near-binary flag can carry
little variance and a great deal of information — it corrects rarity's
mistakes rather than restating them.

`role` is the syntactic position a citation occupies in the proof term:
applied to arguments, passed as an explicit argument, filling a typeclass
slot, or appearing only in a type annotation. It is a fact about **this
proof**, not about the library.

## 2. Depth is redundant given rarity

Within a proof, cited depth and rarity correlate **0.57**. They are
substantially the same signal.

| single signal | score |
|---|---|
| random | 0.470 |
| role alone | 0.747 |
| **depth alone** | **0.783** |
| **rarity alone** | **0.833** |

Rarity beats depth by 0.050 [+0.036, +0.064] — significant. But combining them
buys nothing:

| model | score |
|---|---|
| rarity alone | 0.833 |
| rarity × depth | 0.828 |
| rarity, depth breaks ties | 0.833 |

Building up instead from rarity:

| model | score |
|---|---|
| rarity | 0.833 |
| **rarity × role** | **0.878** |
| rarity × role × statement | 0.878 |
| + depth = the promoted composite | 0.871 |

**`rarity × role` — two factors — beats the promoted four-factor model**
by 0.007 [+0.001, +0.013]. Simpler *and* better.

## 3. The version-stability question, answered properly

`FAILURE_ANALYSIS.md` framed longevity as a possibly disqualifying blocker,
because rarity depends on library-wide citation counts that shift whenever
Mathlib changes. Role and depth are both structural and stable.

| model | uses library counts? | score |
|---|---|---|
| rarity × role | yes | **0.878** |
| promoted composite | yes | 0.871 |
| **role × depth** | **no** | **0.846** |
| role, depth breaks ties | no | 0.846 |
| role alone | no | 0.747 |

**A fully version-stable ranking costs 0.025 [−0.043, −0.006]** against the
promoted model. Real, but far smaller than I implied. Longevity is a priced
trade-off, not a blocker.

One caveat: `role, depth breaks ties` matches `role × depth` on the headline
number (0.846) but is much worse at surfacing major mathematics first
(0.742 vs 0.808) because a three-level lexicographic key is coarse. The
multiplicative form is the better version-stable candidate.

---

## 4. The project lead's specific proposal

"Depth is the right idea, rarity as a tiebreaker" — **depth-led scores 0.791**,
barely above depth alone (0.783). Depth has hundreds of distinct levels, so a
tiebreak almost never fires.

The same *shape* led by role instead reaches **0.846**. The instinct — lead
with one signal, break ties with another — is sound; the choice of lead signal
was the issue, and it was mine to get right, not theirs.
