# Conditional Long-Term Vision

## 1. The vision worth preserving

Mathematics may be fruitfully viewed as an evolving library of named, certified abstractions, together with records of how those abstractions are used in local contexts.

A future system could combine:

- an exact Lean-backed corpus;
- human-scale declaration and concept maps;
- local usage history;
- learned navigation;
- alternative proof routes;
- new definitions and lemmas;
- conjecture and goal formation;
- verification;
- curation.

## 2. Conditional architecture

If the representation studies and navigation experiments are positive, a future object may contain:

\[
\text{map} + \text{experience} + \text{evolution}.
\]

### Map

Named declarations, proof artifacts, selected route projections, semantic concepts, and fold/expand relations.

### Experience

Contexts, goals, chosen tools, instantiations, outcomes, failures, and source explanations.

### Evolution

New definitions, conjectures, proofs, counterexamples, alternative routes, and curation decisions.

## 3. Mathematical roles

The long-term loop remains:

\[
\text{Explore}\to\text{Define}\to\text{Conjecture}\to\text{Shape}\to\text{Prove/Refute}\to\text{Verify}\to\text{Curate}.
\]

Lean supplies verification. Learned systems may eventually share representations across the other roles.

## 4. What is not established

- that one explicit graph is the correct substrate;
- that local theorem-use learning scales to discovery;
- that alternative proofs reveal deep morphisms automatically;
- that compression is a sufficient value function;
- that a shared model can perform all roles;
- that the system will be transformative.

This document preserves direction, not authorization for immediate implementation.
